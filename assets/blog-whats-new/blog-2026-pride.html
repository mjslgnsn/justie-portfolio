Being Me — A Tribute to
My Pride & My Advocacy

Pride Month has always meant more to me than parades and rainbow filters. It's a reminder of how far I've come in owning who I am, and of the work I've chosen to carry — for HIV awareness, for gender-based rights, for a community that deserves to be seen, safe, and celebrated all year round.

This is me — showing up fully, unapologetically, for Pride and for the people I fight for.

This is me

For a long time, being part of the LGBTQ+ community meant learning to take up space in rooms that weren't always built for me. Pride Month is a yearly reminder of that journey — of the fear I had to unlearn, the people who made it safer to be myself, and the version of me who finally stopped shrinking.

I don't just show up for Pride once a year. I show up for it in the work I do, the conversations I start, and the spaces I try to make a little more open for the next person still figuring themselves out.

Standing in spaces I once thought weren't for me.

Being visible isn't always comfortable, but it's necessary. Every time someone sees me living openly, it chips away a little more at the silence that used to protect no one but the discomfort of others.

Why I fight

Advocacy found me the same way most important things do — through people I love getting hurt by systems that weren't built to protect them. Fighting for LGBTQ+ rights isn't abstract to me. It's about the friend who was denied basic respect at a clinic, the sibling still hiding from family, the coworker who has to think twice before mentioning their partner.

So I fight where I can — in conversations, in the organizations I'm part of, in the platforms I have access to, and now, in the corner of the internet that is this blog.

Community work — where the real advocacy happens.

Raising HIV awareness

One of the causes closest to my heart is HIV awareness. Stigma still keeps people from getting tested, from seeking treatment, from even talking about it — and silence has never once made anyone safer. Part of my advocacy is making sure the people around me know that testing is normal, that treatment works, that a diagnosis is not a life sentence, and that no one deserves to be shamed for their status.

Talking openly about HIV — because silence is the real danger.

Gender-based rights for all

My advocacy doesn't stop at my own identity. Gender-based rights are human rights — full stop. That means fighting against discrimination based on gender identity or expression, pushing for policies that protect everyone regardless of who they are or who they love, and reminding people that equality isn't a favor being granted, it's something owed.

This work is for the people who came before me and made it a little safer for someone like me to exist loudly, and for everyone still waiting for the world to catch up.

Fighting for rights that should never have been up for debate.

Pride is protest and celebration

I think that's what I love most about Pride Month — it holds both truths at once. It's a party and a protest. A celebration of how far we've come, and a loud reminder of how far there still is to go. I get to be both proud and unfinished, both joyful and still fighting.

Joyful, proud, and still fighting.

What's next

This blog entry is just one more way of being loud about who I am and what I stand for. I'll keep showing up for HIV awareness campaigns, keep pushing conversations on gender-based rights, and keep making sure the people around me know they don't have to hide to be loved. Pride doesn't end with June — it just gets to be a little louder.
